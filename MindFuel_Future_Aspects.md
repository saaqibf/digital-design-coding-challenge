# MindFuel Future Aspects

Where the Digital Design Coding Challenge can go next. The current tracks are the entry point: zero experience, guided challenges, points instead of judges. Everything below builds on that foundation toward older students, experienced coders, and true open-ended hackathons.

---

## Pathway 1: The Open Challenge (the real hackathon)

For students who have finished any career track, or who arrive with some coding experience. This is the classic hackathon format, stepped down just enough to fit school life:

- **The setup:** students form teams of 2 to 4. They get a problem statement, not instructions. Example: *"Your city's parks are underused in winter. Using any data you can find or invent, build something that helps the city decide what to change."*
- **The time-box:** one school day (5 hours) for the short version, or 2 to 3 days for the full version, which is exactly how Callysto runs its online hackathons (virtual launch event, independent work time, drop-in support sessions, submission for judging).
- **The tools:** anything free and in the browser. Teams that finished a career track already know notebooks, data, and charts. Teams with more experience can go as far as they want. Where schools permit, AI tools are allowed with one rule borrowed from university hackathons: you must understand and be able to explain every line you submit.
- **The finish:** demo day. Each team gets 3 minutes: the problem, the solution, the evidence. Judged on a simple rubric: does it address the problem (10), is it backed by data or working code (10), how creative is the approach (5), quality of the demo (5).
- **Why this works as the next step:** the career tracks teach the skills; the Open Challenge removes the rails. Students who did both have experienced the full arc from "never coded" to "shipped something original in a team under a deadline."

## Pathway 2: Older and experienced students (grades 10-12+)

- **Full-code career tracks:** same seven-challenge structure, but the code cells arrive empty with hints instead of pre-written. Students write the pandas themselves. The build guide makes this a variant, not a rebuild.
- **Real datasets:** swap the fictional data for live open data (data.calgary.ca, data.edmonton.ca, Statistics Canada). The Wildlife track with real park species counts is a different experience for a 17-year-old than fictional habitats.
- **University-style events:** for senior students, a Devpost-listed event with industry mentors is the bridge to the real hackathon circuit, and MindFuel alumni who started on the career tracks would arrive already knowing the shape of the day.

## Pathway 3: The season model

Instead of one event, a points economy across a semester: career tracks, drone missions, and Genius Hours all feed one scoreboard, with small guaranteed rewards for participation rather than winner-takes-all prizes (the model proven at scale by Hack Club's summer programs, where everyone who logs verified hours earns something). This turns one-off events into an ongoing program, which also reads well in grant applications.

## Pathway 4: More careers, more languages, more access

- **The remaining 11 built tracks** (Epidemiologist through Agriculture Scientist) roll onto this repo over time.
- **New tracks** take half a day each using `BUILD_GUIDE_new_tracks.md`. Natural candidates: veterinarian, pilot, geologist, water quality engineer, sports medicine.
- **French versions** open federal funding pathways (CanCode) and francophone schools; with the engine built, translation is the only work.
- **Accessibility pass** (screen readers, colour contrast, dyslexia-friendly fonts in the banners) widens who can play.
- **Measurement:** a two-question confidence survey at the start and end of each event turns every session into evidence for funders.
